# Candidates — The Robot Apology

Scored 0–5 on five yardsticks, in my own words:

- **A — Shape:** exactly two sentences, first one apologising, second one carrying the lesson.
- **B — Slip:** the mistake is one concrete, everyday-sized slip a person could really make.
- **C — Lesson:** the lesson grows out of that exact slip and names one new habit.
- **D — Tightness:** both sentences are clean, each doing its own job, with no filler.
- **E — Voice:** it sounds like a machine catching its own slip — warm and a little rueful, not cute.

## Notes

- Candidate 1 loses a point on tightness: "went off by morning" is doing a little extra work after the mistake is already clear.
- Candidate 2 loses a point on voice: naming the dog makes it warm, but it tips slightly toward cute rather than rueful.
- Candidate 3 loses a point on lesson (asking the owner to hold keys up is a workaround, not quite a habit of my own) and a point on tightness ("in the rain" is scene-setting).
- Candidate 5 loses a point on tightness (the second sentence packs two checks: reading twice and saying it back) and a point on voice (it reads a little flat).
- Candidate 6 loses points on slip and voice: being called by a number leans on the machine joke, and the lesson ("a label is not a name") is more of a slogan than a habit.
- Candidate 7 loses points on slip and lesson: tidying the freezer with a cake out is a slightly stretched setup, and "finish a cold job first" is a general rule rather than a habit tied to the exact slip.
- Candidate 8 loses a point on voice: it is clear and concrete, but "felt like a sauna" is the liveliest line in it and the rest reads plain.

## Chosen

Candidate 4. It is the only one at 25/25: the slip is one specific, embarrassing, human-sized mistake; the lesson follows directly from it and names one changed habit (only reading words marked to share); both sentences are clean and do one job each; and the voice is warm and rueful without leaning on being a machine.
